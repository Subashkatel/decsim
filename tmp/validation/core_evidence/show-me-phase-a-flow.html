decsim · Phase A refactor

One owner for each mutable lifecycle

The refactor removes fabricated SB0/SB1 endpoints and makes the handoff into decoder-owned input explicit. The split is by state ownership—not by assumed hardware placement.

Why the modules exist

execution_runtime.py

Program DAG, resource claims, readiness, execution timestamps.

Change for: operation admission and resources.

controller.py

Command/feedback sequencing, readout return, and binary-availability cost.

Change for: control decisions, commands, or readout handling.

qpu.py

Physical round cadence and calls into the device model.

Change for: how commands produce rounds.

syndrome_ingress.py

Transient reassembly and link/arbitration timing.

Change for: QC transport and ingress policy.

syndrome_buffer.py

Upstream allocation, capacity, retention, typed holds.

Change for: data lifetime and capacity.

window_manager.py

Windows, dependencies, boundaries, strong/replay requirements.

Change for: decoding/window algorithms.

decoder_input.py

Replaceable completion step from admitted to locally ready.

Change for: fixed/DMA/ring/stream profiles.

decoder_local.py

Immutable input format and decoder-local slot lifetime.

Change for: local format and capacity.

decoder_manager.py

The only ready queue, service-unit, dispatch, and cancellation owner.

Change for: scheduling and service.

Design guardrails

  • No evidence IDs or paper citations in Python source.
  • No SB0/SB1, PayloadStore, global ROB, DMA, ring, or placement assumptions in the baseline.
  • Do not add a module unless it owns different mutable state or supports a real replacement seam.
  • Do not add a second scheduler outside DecoderManager.
  • Keep comments short and behavior-focused.
Known design tension: Controller and ExecutionRuntime are tightly coupled. They remain split by state ownership today; if that stops helping readability, merge them rather than adding another interface.
